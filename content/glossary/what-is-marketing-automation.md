# Marketing Automation

**Marketing automation is software that runs marketing tasks, email sequences, lead scoring, retargeting, social posting, on triggers rather than a person sending each one manually.**

Marketing automation predates AI automation by 15 years. The category was built around email: capture a lead, drop them into a list, send a welcome sequence, score them on opens and clicks, hand qualified contacts to sales. Mailchimp, HubSpot, ActiveCampaign and the rest grew on that loop. What's changed in 2026 is the layer on top. HubSpot's [State of Marketing 2026](https://www.hubspot.com/state-of-marketing) reports 80% of marketers now use AI for content creation and 75% for media production, and 61% say the field is going through its biggest disruption in 20 years. The plumbing is the same; the content running through it is now generated.

What good looks like is a small number of workflows that actually fire on the right trigger. Lead capture replies in under five minutes. A welcome sequence over five days with one specific offer. Re-engagement when a lead goes cold. Lead scoring that adds a point for opens and subtracts one for unsubscribes, then notifies sales when the score crosses a threshold. [Salesforce's State of Marketing report](https://www.salesforce.com/marketing/resources/state-of-marketing-report/) shows automated nurture and scoring sit near the top of every high-performer's stack, and the consistent finding across vendor surveys is that ROI compounds inside the first year as the list grows.

Where it sits next to [AI Automation](/glossary/what-is-ai-automation) and [Business Process Automation](/glossary/what-is-business-process-automation): marketing automation is BPA pointed at one team. The triggers are familiar, the tools come from the same vendors, Make and Zapier glue most of it together for SMBs, and the AI layer is moving in fast for drafting copy and scoring intent.
