# Topical Authority

**Topical authority is the depth and breadth of a site's coverage of one subject area, as opposed to domain authority, which is a generic site-wide score. Depth on the topic beats raw site-wide strength on long-tail queries.**

The cleanest way to see it: a Melbourne buyers-agent site with 40 suburb-specific articles, each answering one real question a first-home buyer types, will outrank a national property portal on long-tail buyer-agent queries despite a fraction of the backlinks. The portal has authority across "property". The buyers-agent site has authority on "Melbourne buyers agent", and that's the query the buyer types.

The mechanism is the same in both classical search and AI search. Google's algorithm has been weighting topical depth and internal linking heavily since the Hummingbird and BERT updates pushed it past keyword-matching. LLMs do something analogous: when ChatGPT or Perplexity compile an answer, they favour sources where multiple corroborated pages on the topic cross-reference each other, because that signal is harder to fake than a single well-optimised page. [Semrush's research on topical authority](https://www.semrush.com/blog/topical-authority/) and [Ahrefs' analysis of content depth](https://ahrefs.com/blog/topical-authority/) both land in the same place: coverage of the full question set in a niche outperforms isolated high-quality pages on bigger sites.

The canonical structural reference is HubSpot's [topic-cluster model](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/topic-clusters-seo): one pillar page covering the topic broadly, 8 to 20 supporting articles each answering one specific sub-question, all internally linked back to the pillar and across to siblings. The pillar acts as the entry point; the cluster proves you've thought through the whole topic, not just the headline.

What good looks like in practice: a Brisbane physio-clinic site with one pillar on "physio for runners" and 15 supporting articles, each on a specific injury (ITB syndrome, plantar fasciitis, shin splints, runner's knee, each linked back to the pillar and across to related conditions). That cluster competes for hundreds of long-tail variants and gets cited by AI engines when someone asks "best physio for shin splints Brisbane". The competitor with a stronger domain but one generic "sports injury" page doesn't.

UnderCurrent Automations plans topical clusters as the first move on most engagements, not single articles, as part of [SEO & AI Visibility](/seo-ai-visibility). It's how [classical SEO](/glossary/what-is-seo) and [Answer Engine Optimisation](/glossary/what-is-answer-engine-optimisation) compound, the same content does both jobs when the cluster is the unit.
