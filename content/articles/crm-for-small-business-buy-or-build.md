# Should a Small Business Buy a CRM or Build One?

> **Quick Answer:** **Most small businesses should buy a CRM, and build a small custom one only when the bill outgrows the job.**
> - Buy when a basic plan fits your team
> - Add up seats over three years, not one month
> - Build when the yearly bill tops the build
> - Keep it small and owned

A CRM for small business priced per person per month looks cheap until you add up three years of seats. For some teams, that total costs more than building a small one of their own.

Here's the rule we use for buying or building, with a three-year sum you can check against your own quotes.

## Do you need a CRM for small business at all?

**You need a CRM once customer details live in more than one place and follow-ups start slipping.** A CRM is a single list of every customer and every contact you've had with them. The letters stand for customer relationship management. It shows who you quoted, who you called and who needs a nudge, so nothing lives only in [someone's head or inbox](/blog/how-much-time-tradies-spend-on-admin-australia).

[Xero's guide to CRM for small business](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/crm-for-small-business/) puts the trigger simply: a CRM is the next step when spreadsheets and manual tracking no longer keep up. If your whole customer list fits in your head, you don't need one yet.

When you do, the bigger risk is the team, not the software. A [small business CRM setup guide](https://lunasystems.com.au/blog/small-business-crm-setup-guide) says the most common failure in small teams is one person setting it up and nobody else knowing how it works. Whatever you pick, pick something your whole team will open every day.

## What does a bought CRM cost a small business?

**A bought CRM usually costs a small business $10 to $100 per user each month, plus setup.** That range comes from the same Xero guide. Free plans exist, but they cover contacts and not much else.

The per-user part is what catches owners out. A [2026 comparison of CRMs for Australian businesses](https://onebookplus.com.au/blog/best-crm-small-business-australia) found paid plans commonly run A$14 to A$35 per user a month. One popular sales tier runs to roughly A$155 a seat. That's fine for one user. For a team of six, it adds up fast.

Setup is the other line people miss. A [trade business CRM cost guide](https://blog.kabooyaa.com.au/post/crm-cost-for-trade-business-australia) says some platforms charge $500 to $2,000 for onboarding. Bigger systems can add up to $10,000 for setup, training and connections. Text messages often cost extra too, at 5 to 10 cents each. So price the whole team for the whole term.

## What does a small business CRM cost over three years?

**Compare a small business CRM over three years, because that's where seat fees can overtake a one-off build.** The [business.gov.au guide to digital tools](https://business.gov.au/online-and-digital/digital-tools-for-business) calls this total cost of ownership. Total cost of ownership is the upfront cost plus running costs over the tool's life.

Here's a worked example for a team of six, with numbers you can swap for your own. Both bought rows add $2,000 setup, the top of that guide's onboarding range. The custom row assumes a $15,000 build and 15% yearly upkeep for hosting and fixes.

| Option | How the sum works | Three-year cost |
|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheet and inbox | $0 software, paid for in lost follow-ups | $0 plus missed work |
| Bought CRM, entry plan | 6 users × $35 × 36 months, plus setup | $9,560 |
| Bought CRM, sales tier | 6 users × $155 × 36 months, plus setup | $35,480 |
| Small custom CRM | $15,000 build, plus 3 × $2,250 upkeep | $21,750 |

Divide $21,750 by 36 months and you get about $604 a month. Under that, buy. Well over it, price a small custom one.

## What does it cost to have no CRM at all?

**Having no CRM costs you the jobs that go cold while a quote sits unanswered.** It's the line that never shows up on an invoice, so owners skip it. Put a number on it before you compare any software.

Here's a worked example. Say slow follow-up loses you one $800 job a month.

**What one lost job a month costs (an example)**

- A lost job, **$800**
- Months a year, **12**
- Years, **3**
- = Lost over three years, **$28,800**

That's about three times the entry plan's three-year cost. If quotes go quiet, start with [why tradies lose jobs before quoting](/blog/why-tradies-lose-jobs-before-quoting-australia).

The payoff shows up in keeping customers. A [2006 Macquarie University study of Australian companies](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/crm-software-applications-and-business-performance) found CRM software was used mostly for keeping and growing customers. How well it met customer-retention goals was a significant predictor of profit. Service firms were also more likely to use a CRM than other sectors.

## When should a small business build a custom CRM?

**Build a custom CRM when you'd pay for a big plan just to use three features.** A custom CRM is a customer system built for your business alone. A small one holds your customer list, your follow-up rule and a link to your accounts, and nothing else.

Before you build, try the middle path. At UnderCurrent Automations, it's the first thing we check with Australian owners. [Xero's CRM app listing](https://apps.xero.com/au/function/crm) shows plenty that connect to your Xero account. Some in [one Xero app collection](https://apps.xero.com/au/collection/connect-xero-and-hubspot) sync contacts, invoices and payments both ways, automatically. **Build only if you can tick all five**

- [ ] Your team pays for seats it barely uses
- [ ] You've trialled two bought CRMs and both missed your process
- [ ] Your CRM bill would top about $604 a month
- [ ] You know the one follow-up rule it must run
- [ ] Someone will own the upkeep after launch

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a spreadsheet good enough as a CRM for a small business?**

A spreadsheet is good enough as a CRM for a small business while one person handles every customer. It's free and everyone knows how to use it. It starts failing when two people edit it, when follow-ups need reminders, or when quotes live in email instead. At that point, details drift out of date and jobs go cold. Moving to a free or entry CRM plan is usually the cheapest next step.

**Can a CRM connect to Xero?**

Many CRMs connect to Xero, and Xero lists them in its app store under customer relationship management. A connection usually syncs contacts, and some also sync invoices, quotes and payments both ways. That saves typing the same customer details twice. Check what each connection syncs before you buy, because every app's link to Xero works a little differently. Test it with a few real customers first.

**How long does it take to set up a CRM for a small business?**

Setting up a CRM for a small business takes a few days for the basics and a few weeks to settle in. The basics are importing contacts, setting your stages and adding one follow-up reminder. The settling-in part is getting the whole team to use it daily. Keep the first version simple. Add more fields and automations once everyone is logging calls and quotes without being asked.

**Can I move my customer data out of a CRM later?**

You can usually move your customer data out of a CRM, but check how before you sign up. Most CRMs let you export contacts as a spreadsheet file. Notes, call history, emails and attachments are often harder to export, or come out messy. Ask the provider what a full export includes, and test it during the free trial. Do that before you load in years of customer history.

**What is a custom CRM?**

A custom CRM is customer relationship software built for one business, instead of rented by the seat from a provider. It holds only the fields, stages and follow-up rules that business uses. It costs more upfront, and you pay for its hosting and upkeep each year. The upside is no per-user fees and a process that fits exactly. For most small teams, a bought CRM is the better first step.
