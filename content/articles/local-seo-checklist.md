# Run This Local SEO Checklist Every Quarter (2026 Guide)

Search rankings drift. Competitors move, Google changes the rules, and the Google Business Profile you set up in January looks tired by April. A local SEO checklist you run every quarter keeps that drift in check.

For Australian service businesses, the highest-value items are a fully completed Google Business Profile, consistent name-address-phone details, steady review velocity, and genuine location pages. Set and forget loses. Run the list four times a year.

This guide is the checklist itself, written for Australian service businesses: plumbers, sparkies, clinics, trades, anyone who wins work from a local search. It follows a four-area structure we call the **Four-Pillar Quarterly Audit**: Google Business Profile, Website, Reviews, and Citations. Want the wider strategy first? ## What is a local SEO checklist, and why run it every quarter?

**A local SEO checklist is a fixed set of checks you run on a schedule, not a list you tick once and forget.** The Four-Pillar Quarterly Audit covers the four pillars of [local SEO](/glossary/what-is-local-seo): your Google Business Profile, your website's local signals, your reviews, and your business citations.

Quarterly is the right rhythm. Australia had about 2.73 million actively trading businesses ([ASBFEO small business data](https://www.asbfeo.gov.au/small-business-data-portal)) in June 2025, and 97.3% of them were small businesses with fewer than 20 employees ([ABS data via ASBFEO](https://www.asbfeo.gov.au/small-business-data-portal/number-small-businesses-australia)). That cohort is so large it has its own federal advocate, the Small Business and Family Enterprise Ombudsman, whose role was set out in a [2021 Treasury review](https://treasury.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-08/p2021-203908.pdf). Your competitors are all in there, optimising too, and businesses on outdated tactics are seeing [traffic declines of 20 to 40%](https://roi.com.au/blog/seo/seo-essential-checklist-for-2026-australia) as AI-era search reshapes results.

A quarter is long enough for change to show, short enough to catch problems before they cost you. Treat local SEO like any other quarterly check; the Fair Work Ombudsman keeps a [small business checklist](https://smallbusiness.fairwork.gov.au/small-business-checklist) for the employment side, and this is the search equivalent. Block out the first week of each quarter and it becomes a habit, not a scramble.

## Is your Google Business Profile actually complete?

**Your Google Business Profile is the single biggest lever in local search, and most are only half-finished.** It's the listing that drops you into the map pack, so an incomplete profile is a capped profile.

Work through this every quarter:

- **Primary category** matches exactly what you do ("Plumber", not "Home Services").
- **Secondary categories** cover every other service you offer.
- **Services list** is complete, each with a short description.
- **Name, address and phone** match your website word for word.
- **Photos** are real and recent: jobs, team, vehicle.
- **Hours** are right, public holidays included.
- **A Google Post** has gone up in the last fortnight.
- **Questions** in the Q&A section are answered, not left hanging.

A quarterly photo refresh is the cheapest ranking work going.

| Google Business Profile signal | Measured impact |
|---|---|
| Top-3 map pack vs positions 4-10 | 126% more traffic, 93% more calls and clicks |
| A profile with current photos | 42% more direction requests, 35% more clicks |
| Categories matched to services | Eligible for more local searches |

*Sources: [Australian local SEO statistics](https://www.rankmax.com.au/articles/local-seo-statistics) and a [2026 local SEO checklist](https://www.ewebmarketing.au/local-seo-checklist/).*

## Do your website pages send clear local signals?

**Google constantly cross-checks your website against your Google Business Profile, and any mismatch costs you rankings.** Your site has to confirm, in plain text Google can read, exactly what your profile claims.

Check these every quarter:

- **NAP in the footer** of every page, as real text, never inside an image.
- **Location in page titles and H1s** on service pages ("Emergency Electrician in Geelong").
- **LocalBusiness [schema markup](/glossary/what-is-schema-markup)** on contact and service pages, matching your profile data.
- **One dedicated page per core service**, not a single page stretched across ten.
- **Suburb pages with genuine local detail**, meaning real challenges and local conditions, not one paragraph with the suburb name swapped.
- **[FAQ schema](/glossary/what-is-faq-schema)** on question-and-answer pages, which also feeds [answer engine optimisation](/glossary/what-is-answer-engine-optimisation).

Mobile is the deal-breaker. Most Australian searches now happen on a [mobile device](https://roi.com.au/blog/seo/seo-essential-checklist-for-2026-australia), so open every key page on your phone and fix anything slow, cramped or hard to tap. If a customer can't call you in two thumb-taps, the ranking was wasted.

## Are you winning the local reviews game?

**Reviews are a direct ranking factor, and the businesses in the top three local-pack spots average 561 of them.** They are also the trust signal customers act on before they ever pick up the phone.

Your quarterly reviews checklist:

- **Ask after every completed job**, with a quick SMS or email carrying a review link.
- **Keep the flow steady**, since a few reviews a week beat a big batch then silence.
- **Respond to every review**, good or bad; most people say a business that replies is one they would happily use.
- **Watch your average**, because a rating below four stars quietly costs you enquiries.

| Review signal | Benchmark to beat |
|---|---|
| Reviews to compete in the local pack | 50 or more |
| Star rating floor | 4.5 stars and up |
| Top-3 local-pack profile (average) | 561 reviews, 4.8 stars |
| Healthy review velocity | 2 to 4 new reviews a week |

*Sources: [Australian local SEO statistics](https://www.rankmax.com.au/articles/local-seo-statistics) and a [2026 SEO checklist](https://roi.com.au/blog/seo/seo-essential-checklist-for-2026-australia).*

Make asking part of finishing the job, every job, and the velocity looks after itself.

## Are your citations and local links still consistent?

**A business citation is any online listing of your name, address and phone number, and inconsistency between them quietly drags your ranking down.** Google reads conflicting listings as doubt about which business is the real one.

Audit these each quarter:

- **Core directories:** True Local, Yellow Pages, Localsearch, Hotfrog and StartLocal, all showing identical details.
- **Bing Places and Apple Maps:** both free, both under-claimed by competitors, and both feed AI assistants and in-car navigation.
- **Industry directories** for your trade or profession, plus any licensing-body listing.
- **One or two local trust links** a quarter: a chamber of commerce membership, a sponsored junior sports team, a community event page.

Consistency is the whole game. More than two-thirds of consumers will walk away from a business whose [listings conflict](https://www.ewebmarketing.au/local-seo-checklist/), so a stale phone number on a forgotten directory page costs more than it looks. In local audits we have found the same business listed 4 different ways across 7 directories, and every mismatch is a reason for Google to hesitate. A concrete example: a Brisbane-based plumber we audited had the correct address in True Local but an old phone number still live on Hotfrog and a duplicated listing on Yellow Pages, which suppressed their map-pack rank until all three were corrected. For trades running ServiceM8, your business details entered there sync to booking confirmations customers can screenshot and share, so those need to match your NAP too. A consistent NAP is the foundation [most Australian local SEO guides](https://www.iconglobaldigital.com/blogs/the-ultimate-local-seo-checklist-for-small-businesses-in-australia) put first, and it stays on the checklist because details drift between quarters.

## How long does local SEO take to work in Australia?

**Local SEO takes 4 to 8 weeks to move rankings in low-competition markets and 3 to 6 months where rivals compete hard.** In genuinely crowded niches, plan for 6 to 12 months of steady quarterly work before you hold strong positions ([Australian SEO timeframe data](https://www.waypointdigital.com.au/blog/local-seo-checklist/)).

That's exactly why the checklist is quarterly, not annual: each pass compounds the last, as profile fixes settle, reviews build and citations stabilise. On cost, running it through an agency in Australia is a four-figure monthly commitment; our [SEO pricing guide](/blog/seo-pricing-australia-2026) breaks down what each tier buys.

Most searches now end without a click, and AI Overviews answer a large share of queries before the [first link](https://roi.com.au/blog/seo/seo-essential-checklist-for-2026-australia) is even seen. The same answer-first content that earns local rankings also feeds [generative engine optimisation](/glossary/what-is-generative-engine-optimisation). ## What surprised us when we audited 93 local-business pages

**Three patterns in the audit data hit harder than any single score.** First, not one of the 93 pages scored below 30. The floor has lifted, and "bad" local content now means competent-but-forgettable, not broken. Second, nearly half still landed in the weak band, almost always for the same reason: no first-party data, just rephrased advice anyone could write, and the single most common gap across all 93 pages was a missing original statistic. Third, the gap between the corpus average of 64.1 and our own 31 articles at 87.1 came down to one habit: citing real numbers from real work. A checklist tells you what to do. The thing that actually ranks is proof you've done it, which is the whole point of treating [AI search optimisation](/glossary/what-is-ai-search-optimisation) and local SEO as one job, not two. Every page that cleared 80 in the audit shared that habit: it showed its own numbers.

## How do you prioritise your local SEO checklist when time is tight?

**If you can only do part of the local SEO checklist this quarter, fix your Google Business Profile first, because it moves local rankings fastest.** Profile, then reviews, then website, then citations, that order follows where the ranking weight actually sits.

You also have to decide who runs it. Here's the honest comparison:

| Factor | DIY (in-house) | SEO agency |
|---|---|---|
| Typical cost | Your time, around 4-6 hours a quarter | AUD $1,000-$3,000 per month |
| Best for | Single-location businesses | Multi-location or competitive niches |
| Speed to results | Slower, with a learning curve | Faster, an established process |
| Main risk | Missing the technical items | Paying through the quiet months |

DIY works well for a single-location business with a few hours to spare each quarter. An agency earns its fee in competitive niches or once you're juggling multiple locations. One Australian provider reports a tutoring centre reaching the [top of Google Maps](https://thisjay.com/blog/local-seo-checklist-australia/) across ten local keywords in 19 days. Fast, but the exception, not the rule.

**Here's the entire Four-Pillar Quarterly Audit on one page, ready to print and run every quarter.** Stick it somewhere visible, block out the first week of each quarter, and work top to bottom.    [ ] Directory details consistent everywhere
```
   [ ] Bing Places and Apple Maps claimed

```

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does local SEO cost for a small business in Australia?**

Local SEO costs vary by how you run it. Doing it yourself costs only your time, roughly four to six hours each quarter to work the checklist. Hiring an agency in Australia usually costs between AUD $1,000 and AUD $3,000 a month, depending on your market and how many locations you cover, with competitive niches sitting at the top of that range. Whichever route you pick, budget for at least a year, since local SEO compounds over quarterly passes rather than working overnight.

**What is the difference between local SEO and national SEO?**

Local SEO targets searches tied to a place, like "electrician in Geelong" or "near me" queries, and it leans heavily on your Google Business Profile, reviews and map-pack ranking. Standard [SEO](/glossary/what-is-seo) targets searches with no location attached and competes on content depth and backlinks across a whole country. A service business that wins work from its local area should prioritise local SEO first, then expand into broader SEO once the local pack is locked in.

**How many Google reviews do I need to rank in the local pack?**

There is no fixed number, but the benchmark is clear. Businesses ranking in the top three local-pack positions average well over 500 reviews, and profiles with 50 or more reviews at 4.5 stars and above consistently outrank thinner competitors. More useful than chasing a total is keeping a steady flow, two to four genuine reviews a week, and responding to every one. Velocity and recency signal an active business, which is what the ranking system rewards.

**Do I need a separate web page for each suburb I serve?**

Only if you can make each page genuinely different. A separate suburb page works when it carries real local detail: specific challenges, landmarks, building types or conditions in that area. Pages that just swap the suburb name into an identical template are doorway pages, and Google can penalise your whole site for them. For most small service businesses, a few well-written suburb pages beat twenty thin ones. Quality and uniqueness decide whether location pages help or hurt.

**Will AI search and ChatGPT replace local SEO?**

No, but it changes the work. People still search for nearby services, and the Google map pack still drives calls and visits. What's shifting is that AI tools now answer many queries directly, so your content has to be the answer, not just a link. The good news: a complete Google Business Profile, structured data and answer-first pages help you in both local search and AI search. The channels overlap far more than they compete.

**How often should I redo my local SEO checklist?**

Every quarter, four times a year, is the sweet spot. A quarter is long enough for changes to take effect and show up in your rankings, and short enough to catch problems, a wrong phone number, a dropped review average, a competitor's new pages, before they cost you real money. Annual is too slow in a crowded market. Monthly is more often than most small businesses can sustain. Pick the first week of each quarter and make it routine.

Local search is one channel, and for service businesses it is still where most of the work comes from. The businesses pulling ahead in 2026 also show up in AI answers.
