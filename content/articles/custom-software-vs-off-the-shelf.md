# Should You Pay for Custom Software Development? Mostly No

> **Quick Answer:** **Most Australian businesses with 1 to 50 staff should buy software, not pay for custom software development.**
> - Buy when your process works like everyone else's
> - Connect your tools when they don't talk to each other
> - Build only the small job no tool covers
> - Most owners never need a full build

It's Thursday night and you've got three tabs open: the booking tool, the accounting software and a spreadsheet that glues them together. A developer has quoted you a system that "does it all". The number has a comma in it, and you're wondering if this is the year to stop patching things together.

This page gives you a plain rule for build or buy, the sums to check it, and the questions to ask before you sign anything.

## Do you need custom software development at 1 to 50 staff?

**You probably don't need custom software development to run a business with 1 to 50 staff.** Accounts, payroll, bookings, quotes and [a CRM](/blog/crm-for-small-business-buy-or-build) are solved problems, and the bought tools for them are cheap and well tested. Custom software development is the work of writing software that only your business uses. It's rarely the first move.

You've got three choices here. You can buy a tool, connect the tools you already have, or build something new.

| Choice | When it fits | What it costs you | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy off-the-shelf software | Your process is like most businesses in your trade | A monthly fee per user | You bend your process a little to fit the tool |
| Connect your tools | Two good tools don't share data | A one-off setup, sometimes a small monthly fee | Someone has to watch the connection |
| Build custom software | One weekly job no tool can do | An upfront build plus yearly upkeep | You own every bug, update and fix |

We're UnderCurrent Automations, and we build custom apps for Australian small businesses. Even so, we steer most owners to the first two rows first.

## What does off-the-shelf software cost a small business?

**Off-the-shelf software usually costs a small service business a few hundred dollars a month, all in.** A [2026 cost breakdown for Australian small business](https://onebookplus.com.au/blog/true-cost-software-australian-small-business-2026) put a 5-employee service business at A$621 to A$844 a month. That's about A$7,452 to A$10,128 a year across all their tools.

Most owners plan to spend far less on anything new. The [Square and COSBOA State of Small Business report](https://squareup.com/au/en/press/square-cosboa-state-of-small-business-2025) found most businesses upgrading their tech expect to spend under $5,000 in the next year. A custom build almost never fits inside that.

The same breakdown found 47% of SaaS licences go unused. SaaS is the software you rent monthly instead of owning. So before you build or buy anything new, cancel the seats nobody logs into. The [business.gov.au guide to digital tools](https://business.gov.au/online-and-digital/digital-tools-for-business) says to work out total cost of ownership before you choose. Total cost of ownership is the upfront cost plus running costs over the tool's life.

## What does custom software development cost over three years?

**Custom software development costs the build price plus every year you keep it running, so compare it over three years.** The build quote is only the first bill. Hosting, security updates and small changes follow every year after.

Here's a worked example, with numbers you can swap for your own. Say a team of 10 pays $50 a user a month for a job management tool. Now say a developer quotes $30,000 for a custom version, and upkeep runs 15% of the build each year.

**Buy or build over three years (an example)**

- Bought tool, 10 users x $50 x 36 months, **$18,000**
- Custom build, $30,000 + 3 x $4,500 upkeep, **$43,500**

The upkeep line is the one owners leave out. The Australian Cyber Security Centre's [guide to patching applications](https://www.cyber.gov.au/business-government/small-business-cyber-security/small-business-hub/small-business-cloud-security-guides/technical-example-patch-applications) says internet-facing services should be patched within two weeks of a fix's release. That drops to 48 hours if an exploit exists. With a bought tool, the vendor does that. With custom software, you pay someone to do it, forever.

## When does building beat buying?

**Building beats buying when one job repeats every week, eats real hours, and no tool does it after a fair trial.** If any one of those is missing, buy or connect instead. Not sure which job to look at first? Start with [the simplest small business tasks to automate](/blog/simplest-small-business-automation-tasks-australia-2026).

Doing nothing has a price too, so put a number on it. Here's a worked example. Say someone spends 5 hours a week copying data between two systems, at $45 an hour.

**What copying data by hand costs (an example)**

- Hours a week copying data, **5**
- What that hour costs, **$45**
- Weeks a year, **52**
- = A year, **$11,700**

Time your own jobs with the steps in [what manual processes cost your business](/blog/how-much-are-manual-processes-costing-your-business).

**Before you build anything**

- [ ] The job happens at least once a week
- [ ] You've timed it for two weeks and know the hours
- [ ] You've trialled at least two bought tools and both fell short
- [ ] You can name who will own the software after launch
- [ ] The yearly upkeep fits your budget, not just the build

## Is there a middle path between buying and building?

**The middle path is to buy your main tools and build only the small connection between them.** Most small businesses already own decent tools. The pain comes from two of them that don't share data.

Connected tools pay off. The Square and COSBOA report found businesses with API integrations process 2.6 times more in sales. An API is the door that lets one app read or send data to another, with your permission. Xero says it has [more than 1,000 certified apps](https://blog.xero.com/product-updates/rise-of-the-builders-custom-apps-ai/), and in August 2026 said one in five connections into Xero is now a custom app.

That last number is the one to watch. Businesses aren't replacing their accounting system. They're adding small custom pieces around it, which is what [custom workflows and automation](/automation) look like in practice. If a ready-made connector does the job, start there, with a tool like [n8n or Zapier](/blog/n8n-vs-zapier-australia-small-business) doing the wiring.

## What does a small custom app look like in practice?

**A good small custom app does one boring job on a schedule, beside the tools you already pay for.** It doesn't replace your accounting software. It feeds it, or does the step your software can't.

Here's one we've built for our own business. Invoicing for our founder's own support work repeated every week and every month, for the same client. No bought tool did it the way that work was billed, so we built a small invoicing app. It writes each weekly and monthly invoice on a schedule. It has produced every invoice up to number 51 for that client with no manual step.

The code is nothing special. The app is small, it does one job, and the accounting system stays where it was. That's the shape we suggest to most owners who ask UnderCurrent Automations about a build. If invoicing is your sore spot, start with [getting ready for e-invoicing](/blog/einvoicing-small-business-australia-guide), because the rules may change what you need.

## What should you check before hiring a custom software developer?

**Before hiring a custom software developer, get ownership, upkeep and exit plans in writing.** Start with who owns the code. [IP Australia](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/understanding-ip/who-owns-ip) says plainly that IP created by a contractor belongs to the contractor unless the contract says otherwise. So the contract should assign the code to your business. Don't bank on a tax break either. The [R&D Tax Incentive rules](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/research-and-development-tax-incentive/check-if-you-are-eligible-for-the-randd-tax-incentive/excluded-rd-activities-under-the-rdti) exclude software built mainly for your own internal administration.

**Get these in writing**

- [ ] The code and data belong to your business
- [ ] Who patches it, how fast, and for how much
- [ ] Where it's hosted, with logins in your name
- [ ] How you'd move it to another developer

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between bespoke software and off-the-shelf software?**

Bespoke software is built for one business and fits exactly how it works. Off-the-shelf software is built once and sold to thousands of businesses, usually as a monthly subscription. Bespoke costs more up front and you pay for its upkeep. Off-the-shelf is cheaper and the vendor handles updates, but you adjust your process to fit it. For most small businesses, off-the-shelf covers accounts, payroll and bookings well.

**How long does custom software development take?**

Custom software development takes as long as the scope is wide. A small internal tool that does one job can be ready in weeks. A customer-facing system with logins, payments and reports usually takes months, plus testing. The fastest way to shorten it is to cut the scope to one job and launch that first. You can always add the next job once the first one is earning its keep.

**Is no-code a good alternative to custom software?**

No-code tools are a good alternative to custom software when the job is simple and mostly moves data between apps. They let you build forms, workflows and small databases without writing code. They get harder to manage as the logic grows, and costs can climb with usage. For a small business, a no-code build is often the right first step before paying for anything custom.

**What happens to custom software if the developer goes out of business?**

If your custom software developer goes out of business, you keep the software only if you own the code and control the hosting. Check that the code sits in an account in your business's name. Check you hold the hosting and domain logins too. Ask for short written notes on how it works. With those three, another developer can take it over without starting from scratch.

**Can AI build custom software for a small business?**

AI can now build simple custom software for a small business, and it's getting better quickly. Xero reported in August 2026 that new app registrations rose four times since 2025, as owners and bookkeepers build their own. AI makes a first version fast. Someone still needs to test it, secure it and keep it running, so the upkeep question doesn't go away.
