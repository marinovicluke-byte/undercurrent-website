> - Bid on local, ready-to-book terms only
> - Geo-target a tight 5 to 10km radius
> - Track bookings, never just clicks
>
> Most groomers burn cash on broad keywords and no tracking. Fix those two things first.

Or they never touch paid search because someone told them it doesn't work. Both are wrong. Paid search is one of the few levers a grooming business fully controls, and right now the auction is unusually quiet. ## When Do Google Ads Actually Make Sense for a Groomer?

**Google Ads for dog grooming make sense when you have empty slots to fill, not when your book is already full.** Paid search buys attention you cannot earn fast enough any other way. If you are already turning dogs away, every dollar buys a booking you would have got for free. So before you open an ad account, be honest about your real capacity. There are three clean cases where the maths usually works. A brand-new salon nobody has heard of yet, where organic visibility takes months to build. A quiet patch, like the slow weeks after the Christmas rush, where you want to top up the diary. And a mobile expansion, where you are pushing your van into new suburbs and need pet owners there to know you exist. In each case you have a specific gap and a specific window, which is what paid search fills well. Demand is not the problem here: with [2,729,648 actively trading businesses recorded in Australia at June 2025](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/latest-release), competition for local customers is fierce, and an invisible grooming business loses the booking to the salon that showed up. Our guide to [SEO for small business](/blog/seo-for-small-business) covers the slower, free side of getting found, and [SEO for dog groomers](/blog/seo-for-dog-groomers) covers the local results paid search sits on top of.

## What Is Google Ads for Dog Grooming, and How Does It Pay Back?

**Google Ads for dog grooming is paid search: you bid to appear at the top of Google the moment a local pet owner searches, and you only pay when someone clicks.** Unlike free rankings, it is instant and rented, switch it on and you show today, switch it off and you vanish. The whole game is winning clicks from people ready to book and turning enough of them into dogs in the chair to clear the spend. The number that matters is cost per booking, not click price. With an [average grooming ticket of about $95 in Australia](https://www.woofspark.com.au/is-dog-grooming-profitable-australia/), you can pay a fair bit per click and still profit, as long as enough clicks become booked dogs. That margin is also why paid search beats the free local pack in one specific way: a [Google Business Profile](/glossary/what-is-google-business-profile) ranks heavily on how close you sit to the searcher, so ads are one of the few ways to appear above a nearer rival you cannot out-distance organically. Done right it is the paid chapter of a wider plan, sitting alongside [SEO versus Google Ads for dog grooming](/blog/seo-vs-google-ads-dog-grooming) and a [booking-ready website](/blog/pet-grooming-website-design).

## How Much Should a Dog Groomer Spend on Google Ads?

**A solo groomer should start on $10 to $20 a day, around $300 to $500 a month, and only scale once the booking maths works.** That range is what grooming-business guides recommend for a single operator, per [The Groomer's Guide to Google Ads](https://www.thedailygroomer.com/blog/the-groomers-guide-to-google-ads) and [Try Teddy](https://tryteddy.com/blog/google-ads-a-primer-for-growing-grooming-businesses). Start small for the data: you cannot tell a winner from a money pit until you have a few hundred clicks, so learn that on $300, not $3,000. Watch your turnover too, because GST registration kicks in once you pass [the $75,000 threshold set by the ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst), which changes your real margin per groom.

## What Does Google Ads Cost Per Click for Local Dog Grooming?

**Cost per click for local grooming terms usually sits in the low single dollars, and a hot "near me" search in a big city can spike higher.** The good news for groomers is the auction is quiet, so clicks stay cheap. But the click price is not the number that decides profit, your cost per booking is. Work it backwards from the [average $95 grooming ticket](https://www.woofspark.com.au/is-dog-grooming-profitable-australia/): if a $4 click converts to a booking one time in ten, that is $40 to win a $95 groom, which works. The same $4 click on a broad term that converts one time in fifty costs $200 a booking, which does not. Two levers pull the click price down. Quality Score, the relevance rating Google gives each keyword, lowers your cost when your ad and landing page match the search. And [Google's ad auction uses both your bid and your ad quality to set position](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375454), so you often pay less than your maximum bid. Tight, high-intent keywords lift quality and cut the price, which is why the next section matters most.

## What Are the Best Keywords for a Grooming Google Ads Campaign?

**Bid only on local, high-intent terms a ready customer types, then block the rest with negatives, because broad keywords are what drain most grooming budgets.** Bid on the bare word "dog grooming" and you pay for every search under the sun, including people hunting grooming jobs, courses, and kits to do it themselves. None of them book you. Group keywords by the way people search: a "near me" group for terms like "dog groomer near me" and "mobile dog grooming", a suburb group pairing your area with "dog groomer", and a service group for breed and treatment terms like "poodle grooming" or "cat grooming". Then add [negative keywords](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972), which tell Google which searches to skip. Here is a starter negative-keyword list you can paste straight into a new campaign:

```text
-jobs
-job
-career
-salary
-course
-courses
-training
-school
-free
-diy
-"how to"
-kit
-clippers
-equipment
-wholesale
```

That short list alone stops a big chunk of wasted spend. Tight match types are not an advanced extra, they are the line between a profitable campaign and an expensive hobby.

## How Do You Set Up a Grooming Google Ads Campaign?

**Build one tight Search campaign around local, high-intent searches, split into three or four focused ad groups, each pointing at a page that lets people book.** Keep it simple at the start, because a single campaign you can manage beats a sprawling account you cannot. Use the same groups you built your keywords around, a "near me" group, a suburb group, and a service group, and give each its own tight keyword set and an ad that mirrors the search. A higher Quality Score lowers your cost, and matching ad to query is how you earn it. Point every click at a booking page, not a homepage they have to dig through, because the same answer-first discipline that wins the [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist) wins paid clicks too. Add call and location assets so a pet owner can ring or get directions straight from the ad. Then track three actions before you spend real money: phone calls from the ad, form submissions, and clicks through to your booking tool. Get the [foundations of a booking-ready website](/blog/pet-grooming-website-design) right first, because paid traffic only converts as well as the page it lands on.

## How Tight Should Your Geo-Targeting Be?

**Set a tight radius of 5 to 10 kilometres, because grooming is a local, proximity-driven service.** This is where most groomers leave money on the table. They target a whole city to "reach more people", then pay for clicks from pet owners 40 minutes away who will never make the drive. Grooming bookings cluster close to home, so your ads should too. [Google's location targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043) lets you draw a radius around your salon address, then exclude the suburbs you will not service. Add call and location assets so a pet owner can ring or get directions straight from the ad, a setup the AU guide at [Extra Strength](https://www.extrastrength.com.au/article/google-ads-for-pet-grooming-services-building-a-steady-customer-base) also pushes. Tight targeting cuts wasted spend and lifts your relevance to the people who can book. It matters even more once you understand the free local results: a [Google Business Profile](/glossary/what-is-google-business-profile) ranks heavily on proximity, so paid search is one of the few ways to appear above a nearer rival. ## How Should a Mobile Dog Groomer Run Google Ads?

**A mobile groomer should match the ad radius to where the van genuinely drives, then exclude every suburb outside that range, because paid search is the one lever that fills the gaps the free map pack leaves.** Mobile is where ads earn their keep. A service-area Google Business Profile rarely ranks evenly across a wide region, and we have watched a mobile groomer vanish entirely from the free results in suburbs it actively serviced, because the van sat too far from those grid points at measurement. Ads do not have that ceiling. In [Google's location targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722043) you draw the radius around where you actually travel, not a hopeful 40km, and lean on the "near me" and suburb keyword groups so you only pay for owners inside your real run. Send the click to a page that names the suburbs you cover and lets them book. As you push the van into new suburbs, widen the radius a postcode at a time and watch the cost per booking before you commit budget. ## Google Ads or Facebook Ads: Which Works for Dog Groomers?

**Google Ads catch pet owners already searching for a groomer, Facebook ads interrupt people who are not, so for bookings now, paid search wins and Facebook supports it.** The difference is intent. Someone typing "dog groomer near me" into Google has decided they need a groom and is choosing who to call, that is the warmest traffic a salon can buy. A Facebook user scrolling their feed is not looking for you, so the ad has to create the want before it can win the booking, which works for awareness and offers but converts more slowly. For a groomer with empty slots this week, Google Ads put you in front of ready buyers today, which is why this guide leads with paid search. Facebook earns its place for retargeting, showing your salon to people who visited your booking page but did not book, and for promoting a quiet-season offer to a local audience. Run Google first to capture demand, add Facebook to build it. ## What Did We Find Researching Grooming Search in Australia?

**When we audited pet grooming businesses, the paid-search auction looked wide open, and that is the real opportunity right now.** Across the grooming marketing and ads searches we reviewed, we kept seeing the same thing: no paid ads competing and no local pack on the business-facing terms, with results dominated by Reddit, Facebook and Pinterest threads instead. An empty auction means cheaper clicks, because you are not bidding against a wall of other groomers. The second thing we observed reframes why paid even matters: looking at who actually held the top map-pack spots, proximity dominated, and a mobile groomer we looked at vanished entirely from the results in suburbs it actively serviced. That is the catch with the free local pack, if a closer rival sits between you and the customer, you often cannot out-rank them no matter how good you are. Paid search is the one lever that puts you above a nearer competitor today. The window is unusual: low competition on the ads, a proximity ceiling on the free results.

## How Do You Track Bookings, Not Just Clicks?

**Judge every campaign on cost per booking, because clicks do not pay your rent.** This is the discipline that separates groomers who profit from paid search from those who quietly subsidise Google. Conversion tracking ties a click to a real enquiry, and you want three actions live before you spend real money: track phone calls from the ad, form submissions on your booking page, and clicks through to your booking tool like MoeGo. Then each week, look at one number per campaign: what did a booked dog cost? Pair that with [Google's geographic performance report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453994) to see which suburbs actually convert, so you can shift budget toward the postcodes that book. If a "near me" group brings $4 clicks that turn into $95 bookings, keep feeding it. If a service group burns $60 in clicks for zero bookings, pause it. No-shows make this sharper, because a no-show on a $100 groom costs the full $100 plus the empty slot, so you want ads that bring reliable, local, ready-to-book customers. The automation that confirms and reminds those bookings is its own subject, covered in [AI automation for pet grooming](/blog/ai-automation-for-pet-grooming).

## Should You Run Google Ads or Build SEO First?

**Run a small ad budget for bookings now, build SEO and your profile for free bookings later, and most groomers do both.** Be clear on what each tool does. Google Ads is instant and rented: switch it on, appear at the top today, switch it off, and you vanish. SEO is the slow, owned work of earning free rankings, and together with your [local SEO](/glossary/what-is-local-seo) profile it brings bookings month after month without a daily spend. If you need dogs in the chair this week, ads win. If you want a business that does not depend on a meter running, the organic work wins. For most growing groomers the answer is a sequence, not a choice: a modest ad budget to fill the diary now while the slower search work compounds underneath it. ### How do I set up Google Ads geo-targeting for a dog grooming business?

Set a tight radius around your salon or service area, usually 5 to 10 kilometres, rather than a whole city, because most bookings come from nearby. In Google Ads, choose location targeting, drop a radius around your address, and exclude suburbs you will not travel to. Mobile groomers should match the radius to where they genuinely drive. Tight targeting wastes less money and lifts your relevance to local searchers.

### Google Ads or SEO first for a dog grooming business?

If you need bookings this week, start with Google Ads, because it puts you at the top of the page the day you switch it on. If you want durable, free visibility that compounds over months, invest in SEO and your Google Business Profile. Most growing groomers run a small ad budget for instant flow while the slower local-search work builds underneath it. They solve different problems, so the smart move is usually both, in order.

### How do I track bookings from Google Ads as a dog groomer?

Track real bookings, not clicks. Turn on conversion tracking for phone calls from the ad, form submissions on your booking page, and clicks through to your booking tool. In Google Ads, set up call reporting and a conversion action for completed enquiries, then judge each campaign on cost per booking. A campaign with cheap clicks but no bookings is losing money, so the booking number is the only one that decides whether to keep spending.
