# How Many Small Businesses Use AI in Australia in 2026?

> **Quick Answer:** **AI in Australia is used by about 44% of small and medium businesses, says the government's own monthly survey.**
> - The ABS puts small and micro firms near 11%
> - The gap comes from how each survey asks
> - Most use it for writing and data
> - Few have changed how work runs

One government survey says 44% of small and medium businesses use AI. The ABS puts small and micro firms nearer 11%, and both numbers can be right.

Here's what the government numbers say about AI in Australia, and where small businesses get stuck.

## What does AI in Australia look like for small business in 2026?

**AI in Australia is common but shallow in small business. The National AI Centre found 44% of small and medium businesses were adopting AI in February 2026.** That's up from 41% in the tracker's [June 2025 release](https://www.minister.industry.gov.au/t-ayres/media/australian-ai-adoption-tracker-report-shows-business-harnessing-ai).

That figure comes from the centre's [SME AI Pulse for December 2025 to February 2026](https://www.ai.gov.au/news-and-insights/blog/ai-adoption-insights-december-2025-february-2026), first published on 7 May 2026. The SME AI Pulse is a monthly government survey of at least 400 small and medium business owners. Across the whole quarter it was 43%, down slightly from 45% the quarter before. So the growth has slowed, even if the headline still sounds big.

The more useful detail sits inside the adopters. The centre says limited, one-off use is falling and broad use across the business is at a 7-month high. Firms that commit to AI tend to go deeper. The ones that only dabble tend to stall, which is the same pattern [UnderCurrent Automations](/ai-automation-australia) sees in Australian owners who ask for help.

## Why do the ABS and the National AI Centre disagree on AI in Australia?

**The two numbers measure different things. The ABS asks if a business used AI at work over a full year, while the tracker asks owners to rate their own adoption.**

The [ABS media release](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/business-adoption-artificial-intelligence-accelerates-2024-25), released 25 June 2026, says about 12% of all Australian businesses used AI in 2024–25. For small and micro businesses it was around 11%.

| Source | What it asks | Who it covers | Small business figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| National AI Centre SME AI Pulse | How owners rate their own AI use | 400+ firms a month | 44% (February 2026) |
| ABS Business Characteristics Survey | Did the business use AI at work | Nearly 7,000 firms, all sizes | About 11% (2024–25) |
| ABS, innovation-active small firms | Same, for firms that improved something | Small firms only | 19%, versus 4% |

Neither number is wrong. The tracker counts an owner who "uses ChatGPT a bit", while the ABS asks a stricter question. Many owners have tried AI, and far fewer have built it into how the business runs.

## Which industries use AI the most in Australia?

**Information, media and telecommunications leads AI use in Australia at 38%, according to the [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/business-adoption-artificial-intelligence-accelerates-2024-25). Professional, scientific and technical services, plus financial and insurance services, sit at 24% each.**

The same data shows size matters a lot. About 35% of large businesses use AI, against 22% of medium businesses. Small and micro firms trail well behind them at around 11%. In the same [ABS data](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/business-adoption-artificial-intelligence-accelerates-2024-25), small firms that had improved a product or process were at 19%, almost five times the 4% of those that hadn't.

The National AI Centre's monthly data tells a similar story by sector. Health, education and services firms lead, with more than half using AI. Construction and agriculture are under 30%. If you run a trade business, you're in the slowest group, so there's still room to get ahead.

## Why are most small businesses still not using AI?

**Trust is the biggest barrier. About 65% of small and medium businesses not using AI said they distrust its decisions or want to keep human control.**

That finding is from the same National AI Centre quarterly report. The next barrier was relevance, with 54% of non-adopters saying AI isn't relevant to their business. Another 19% of all small and medium firms said they simply don't know how to use it, up 2% on the quarter before.

Those three reasons have one thing in common. Most owners have only seen AI as a chatbot that writes text. They haven't seen it read their inbox, update their job sheet or send the invoice. The centre's own report says businesses need examples that look like their own. The fix is to map AI to the jobs your team already does each week.

## What are Australian businesses actually using AI for?

**Writing and data analysis come first. Among small and medium businesses using or planning AI, 54% use it for content creation and the same share for data analytics.**

Cyber security and threat detection come next at 48%. **Agentic AI** is software that takes several steps on its own to finish a job. It's still largely untapped. So is AI for supply chains or HR. The centre puts this down to low awareness and low confidence.

Safeguards are patchy too. About half of current users check AI output before it reaches a customer. Far fewer tell customers when AI is involved or give them a way to raise a concern. If that's you, [business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/online-and-digital/artificial-intelligence) says to make one senior person accountable for AI and write down what it can and can't be used for.

## What changes when buyers start asking AI instead of Google?

**Your customers are already using AI to find businesses like yours, even if your team isn't. We track it on our own site, and the change is fast.**

UnderCurrent Automations is a small Australian business too. In August 2026, visits to our own site from AI assistants went from 3 to 28 sessions in a month. Of those 28, 24 came from ChatGPT. Nobody clicked an ad. In each case, an assistant read our pages, answered someone's question and sent them to us.

That's the part the surveys don't measure. The question for an owner isn't only "do we use AI?" It's also "can AI find and explain us?" Pages that answer a question clearly get cited. Pages that bury the answer get skipped, which is why [AI search works differently from classic search](/blog/ai-search-vs-traditional-search-australia-2026).

## What does it cost to stay at "we tried ChatGPT once"?

**Staying at the trial stage keeps every repeat admin job manual, and those hours add up. Here's a simple example you can check against your own week.**

Say you spend 5 hours a week on quotes, follow-up emails and chasing invoices.

**What the trial stage costs in a year (an example)**

- Hours a week on quotes, follow-ups and invoices, **5**
- What your hour is worth, **$80**
- Weeks a year, **52**
- = Your time a year, **$20,800**

This is an example, not a client result, so swap in your own hours and rate. If two people share that work, double it.

Now picture one of those jobs running on its own. Quote requests get logged, a draft goes out for your review, and the follow-up sends itself on day three. Owners we've worked with usually start there, with [one process mapped end to end](/blog/automating-business-processes-australia-sme-guide) before they buy any new tool.

## Where can Australian small businesses get free help with AI?

**The government's free help sits on business.gov.au and the National AI Centre site. Both give plain guides, templates and safety rules for small firms.**

These are the four free guides we tell Australian owners to read first:

- [ ] The [Guidance for AI Adoption: Foundations](https://www.ai.gov.au/staying-safe-and-responsible/essential-ai-practices/guidance-ai-adoption-foundations), for firms starting out
- [ ] The free [AI policy template](https://www.ai.gov.au/staying-safe-and-responsible/essential-ai-practices/create-ai-policy) from the National AI Centre
- [ ] The [AI register template](https://www.ai.gov.au/staying-safe-and-responsible/essential-ai-practices/ai-systems-register), to list every AI tool you use
- [ ] Safe Work Australia's page on [AI risks at work](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/hazards/digital-technologies-ai)

The wider plan is the [National AI Plan](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/national-ai-plan), launched 2 December 2025. One of its three goals is spreading AI use across every region and industry. ## Where should a small business start with AI in Australia?

**Start with the one repeat job that costs you the most hours each week, not with a new tool.** That's the rule we apply with every Australian owner. The tool choice is easy once the job, its steps and its hours are clear.

**The first-month plan**

1. Write down every job your team repeats each week.
2. Time the three biggest for two weeks.
3. Pick the one with the most hours and the clearest steps.
4. Fix that one before you look at anything else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it safe to put customer details into AI tools?**

Putting customer details into an AI tool is only safe once you know where that data goes. business.gov.au says to check what data the tool uses, how it's stored, and that it protects your customers' information before you rely on it. For an Australian small business, that means using a business account, not a personal one, and writing down which customer details staff may never paste in.

**What is the AI adoption rate in Australia for all businesses?**

The ABS found about 12% of all Australian businesses used AI in their workplace in 2024–25, in data released in June 2026. That's up from very low rates in 2021–22, before tools like ChatGPT were available. Large businesses were at about 35% and medium businesses at 22%. Small and micro businesses were at around 11%, just under the national figure.

**What is the National AI Centre AI Adoption Tracker?**

The AI Adoption Tracker is a government survey run monthly by the National AI Centre in the Department of Industry, Science and Resources. A research firm surveys at least 400 small and medium business owners each month. Results are weighted by industry, state and business size. It's the most current public source on how Australian small businesses use AI, and it reports results each quarter.

**Do small businesses need an AI policy in Australia?**

business.gov.au suggests every small business capture its AI rules in a short policy. It lists writing down what staff can and can't use AI for, who is accountable, and how customer data is protected. The National AI Centre offers a free AI policy template. One page is enough for most small Australian businesses, and it stops staff guessing what's allowed.

**What is agentic AI for a small business?**

Agentic AI is software that takes several steps on its own to finish a task, rather than answering one prompt. For a small business, that could mean reading a quote request, checking a price list and drafting a reply for review. The National AI Centre says Australian small businesses have barely started using agentic AI. Most are still using AI to write content.
