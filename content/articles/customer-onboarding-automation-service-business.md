# Set Up Customer Onboarding Automation for Service Businesses

> **Quick Answer:** **Onboarding automation for service businesses sends the welcome, deposit invoice, booking link, intake form and contract the moment a client says yes.**
> - One trigger starts every step, so nothing waits on you
> - Reminders chase anything the client hasn't done
> - You keep the kickoff call personal
> - Start with the deposit invoice

Winning the quote is the easy part. Then come five more emails, and without customer onboarding automation you send every one by hand.

This guide shows what to automate, what to keep human, and how to set it up in a week.

## What is onboarding automation for service businesses?

**Onboarding automation for service businesses is a set of steps that run by themselves once a client accepts your quote.** One trigger, like a deal marked "won" in your CRM, sends the welcome, the deposit invoice, the booking link, the intake form and the contract.

Each customer onboarding step is a small job you already do by hand. The difference is that the system does it within minutes, in the same order, for every client. It's a type of [workflow automation](/glossary/what-is-workflow-automation), which means software that moves information between your apps and does the next step for you. UnderCurrent Automations builds these sequences for [Australian service businesses](/ai-automation-australia) on the tools they already use.

| Step | Done by hand | Automated |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome message | Sent when you remember | Sent minutes after the client says yes |
| Deposit invoice | Typed into Xero that night | Created from the quote, with a pay link |
| Kickoff booking | Back-and-forth emails | A link showing your real free times |
| Intake form | Questions spread over five emails | One form, saved to the client record |
| Contract or scope | Printed, signed, scanned | Pre-filled and sent for e-signature |
| Reminders | You chase, or forget to | Sent on a timer until it's done |

## What does slow onboarding cost a service business?

**Slow onboarding costs you hours of admin per client and delays the day the deposit lands.** Every chase email is time you can't bill, and every day of delay pushes back the start of the job. Here's a worked example, not a client result, so swap in your own numbers.

**What onboarding admin costs in a year (an example)**

- Hours of admin per new client, **1.5**
- New clients a month, **8**
- Months a year, **12**
- What an hour of your time is worth, **$90**
- = Your onboarding admin a year, **$12,960**

Most of that time goes on copying, chasing and checking. With a sequence running, it shrinks to the kickoff call and a quick look at anything flagged. [business.gov.au says](https://business.gov.au/finance/payments-and-invoicing/payment-terms) clear payment terms help you get paid on time, and your deposit invoice now goes out on day one. That's good for [cash flow](/blog/how-overdue-invoices-hurt-australian-sme-cash-flow). Not sure of your own numbers? ## What does a seven-day onboarding sequence look like?

**A good onboarding sequence takes a client from "yes" to a started job in about a week.** Each step fires on its own day, so the client never gets six emails at once.

**The seven-day customer onboarding sequence**

1. Send a welcome text or email with their name and the job they asked about. **Day 0**
2. Create the deposit invoice from the accepted quote and send it with a pay link. **Day 0**
3. Send the intake form for address, access notes, ABN and any files. **Day 1**
4. Send a booking link for the kickoff call. **Day 2**
5. Remind them about the deposit if it's still unpaid. **Day 3**
6. Send the pre-filled scope or contract for e-signature. **Day 4**
7. Run the kickoff call, then create the job card and tell your team. **Day 5 to 7**

An **intake form** is a short online form that collects what you need to start the job. The order matters more than the tools. Money and dates come first, because they decide when the job can start. Long forms and contracts come once the client has already committed.

## How do I automate customer onboarding follow-ups without a CRM?

**You can automate customer onboarding follow-ups without a CRM by starting the sequence from a form, a spreadsheet row or an accepted quote.** A connector tool like Zapier or Make watches for that event and sends each follow-up on a timer. Leave 24 hours or more between reminders, so clients don't feel hounded. [Zapier's free plan](https://zapier.com/pricing) includes 100 tasks a month, enough to test one sequence.

Your accounting software can also do the chasing. Xero can [send invoice reminders automatically](https://www.xero.com/au/accounting-software/send-invoices/), before and after the due date and weekly until paid. Xero says its customers who take online invoice payments get paid up to twice as fast. A shared spreadsheet then tracks the rest, with one row per client and a column for each step.

A CRM earns its place once you have more than a handful of clients onboarding at once. It keeps every file and message on the client record, so anyone in your team can see where a job is up to. Until then, a spreadsheet and your accounting software can run automated customer onboarding on their own. ## What should you automate, and what should stay human?

**Automate the admin that's the same for every client, and keep a person on anything that needs judgement.** Clients notice a fast, tidy process, but they still want a person for the big decisions. A template still sounds like you when it uses their first name and their actual job. If a step changes from client to client, it isn't ready to automate yet.

**Let the system send**

- The welcome message and deposit invoice
- Booking links and calendar invites
- Intake forms and file requests
- Reminders for anything unpaid or unsigned
- The job card and team alert once the deposit lands

**Keep a person on**

- The kickoff call
- Scope changes and pricing questions
- A client who has gone quiet after three reminders
- Anything that feels like a complaint
- Requests to change the deposit or payment terms

## How do you handle ABNs, documents and client privacy?

**Collect documents through a form that saves straight to the client record, not through email attachments.** Files stuck in an inbox are hard to find later and easy to leak.

You can check a client's ABN as they type it. The [ABN Lookup web services](https://abr.business.gov.au/Tools/WebServices) let a form validate an ABN and pre-fill the details, and access is free. For contracts, the [Electronic Transactions Act 1999](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00553/latest/text) sets out when an electronic signature meets a legal signing requirement under Commonwealth law.

Privacy depends on your size. The OAIC says most [small businesses with turnover of $3 million or less](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business) aren't covered by the Privacy Act, but some are. If you are covered, [APP 11](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-quick-reference) says you must take reasonable steps to protect the personal information you hold. Marketing messages also need consent and an easy way to [unsubscribe under spam law](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How is service business onboarding different from SaaS onboarding?**

Service business onboarding locks in a job, while SaaS onboarding teaches someone to use a product. A software company wants new users to log in and keep using its app. A service business wants the deposit paid, the scope signed and the first visit booked. The tools look similar, but the goal is different. Copying a long SaaS email drip makes customer onboarding feel slow, so keep the sequence short.

**What questions should a client intake form ask?**

A client intake form should only ask what you need to start the job. For most service businesses that means contact details, the site address, access notes, the ABN for business clients and any photos or plans. Ask each question once, then save the answers to the client record in your CRM or spreadsheet. Skip anything you won't use in the first week, because every extra field makes the form slower to finish.

**Do Australian businesses need W-9 forms for client onboarding?**

No, Australian service businesses don't collect W-9 forms during client onboarding. A W-9 is a United States tax form, so it only applies to dealings with the US tax system. The closest local step is collecting a business client's ABN. You can check that ABN against the Australian Business Register's free ABN Lookup service. An intake form can check the number as the client types it, so the details on your invoice are right.

**Does onboarding automation work for high-ticket work?**

Yes, onboarding automation works for high-ticket work, and it often matters more there. A client spending a large sum is watching closely for signs that you're organised. Fast invoices, a clear booking link and a tidy contract show that before any work starts. The work itself stays custom. Only the admin around it gets automated, so the client still gets your full attention where it counts.

**How much does onboarding automation cost to run?**

Onboarding automation can cost very little to run, because most of it uses software you already pay for. Xero can send invoices and automatic payment reminders. Zapier's free plan includes 100 tasks a month, and paid plans add more tasks. The bigger cost is setting it up well. Map your process first, so you only pay for the steps you actually need.
