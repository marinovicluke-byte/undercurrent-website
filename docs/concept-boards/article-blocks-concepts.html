19 looks, five blocks, cut from the live site

Round 2 dressed the blocks in BRAND.md. This round takes undercurrentautomations.com as the design (docs/blocks-design-notes.md): Inter, the one hairline, square corners, the grain cross, the hero's striped lettering, colour only at the size of a mark. Every look is new and rests on its own idea, written under its name. Swap a look's wrapper for another's and the idea still has to be different.

What Ledger, Ramp and Pills had in common: nothing boxed, the hairline as the only structure, and the type (a numeral, a label) carrying the order and the verdict. Every look here starts from that.

Each frame is the article page's own CSS around copy lifted from live UC articles, run through the real transform. The frames were rendered by the round 3 transform and are kept as they were. Real <ol>, <table> and <p><strong> in every look, phone and desktop by CSS alone, nothing hidden behind a tap.

01Steps, four looks

Steps

An ordered list under a bold title; a trailing bold is the step's timing. Copy: the proof article's three steps, then the ranking window (does-chatgpt-search-the-web), the only steps on the site with timings.

Pick: Fill. It keeps what you liked in Ledger and Ramp, a numeral column on hairlines and order you can see, and draws it in the hero's own lettering. No other site has that numeral.

Fill

Pickucb-steps--fill

The numerals are the hero's striped lettering, and each fills from the foot in the article's colour as far as the run has gone: a third full at step one of three, solid at the last.

Honest: The colour is the biggest on the paper after the hero; past eight steps the fills differ by a sliver.

Relay

ucb-steps--relay

The steps are the service page's problem grid read left to right, the grain cross on the hairline where one step hands to the next.

Honest: The idea lives on a wide screen. On a phone the cells stack and it is a numbered list with crosses on its rules.

Command

ucb-steps--command

Each step is set as a headline and its number shrinks to a mark in the margin, so a short run reads as orders, not a list.

Honest: Built for steps of one line. The longer recipe steps on the site would turn into walls of big type.

Signal

ucb-steps--signal

No numerals at all: every step carries the whole run as the carousel's dots, the ones behind it in ink, its own in the colour, the ones ahead in grey.

Honest: You can't say "step three" out loud, and past seven steps the dots run long on a phone.

02Pair, four looks

Pair

Two titled lists in a row. When the titles say before and after, works and doesn't, do and don't, one side is the better side. Free and Paid are equals and get no verdict. Copy: overdue invoices (a verdict), then eInvoicing options (equals).

Pick: Weight. The verdict comes from the type alone, so it reads in a glance with nothing boxed; both sides stay true and legible, and a pair of equals stays equal. Pills, minus the pills.

Strike

ucb-pairs--strike

The worse side is crossed out like an edit and the better side is left standing, so the verdict is in the text, not in a colour.

Honest: Struck text is slower to read, and the "before" list is still worth reading.

Weight

Pickucb-pairs--weight

The verdict is the type: the better side set large in ink and given more of the width, the other dropped to small grey.

Honest: The small side is genuinely small at 14px; with equals it is two plain lists.

Across

ucb-pairs--across

Read line against line: the two lists share one set of rows, so each line sits beside its counterpart, on a phone too.

Honest: Lines that don't correspond still sit side by side as if they did, and on a phone each column is 160px wide.

Signed

ucb-pairs--signed

Every line carries a sign, the grain cross for the side that helps and a dash for the side that costs, so one line quoted alone still says which side it was on.

Honest: The sign is drawn, not written, so an engine quoting a line gets the words without the sign.

03Data table, four looks

Data table

Always a real <table>: row headers, labelled cells, the wrapper a focusable scroller, every view CSS only. Copy: the proof article's comparison (its pick, AI automation, set in bold in the markdown) and the model cutoffs (nine rows).

Pick: Rail. The snap rail you asked for, done the other way round: the row names stay put and the options snap past, so a four-column table reads on a phone with nothing squeezed.

Rail

Pickucb-data--rail

On a phone the row names hold still and the options snap past one column at a time, each column numbered, a hairline filling as you swipe.

Honest: You compare one option at a time on a phone, never two side by side; from 761px it is the plain table.

Ledger

ucb-data--ledger

Read it as an account book: figures in tabular numerals and right-aligned, a rule down every column, a double rule to close, on the grain like paper.

Honest: Only earns its keep on figures; the cutoffs table is all dates and words. Scrolls sideways on a phone.

Pick

ucb-data--pick

One column is the answer. A bold header in the markdown marks it, it reads in full ink under the colour's underline while the rest recede, and on a phone the pick leads every row.

Honest: Needs the writer to bold the pick. A table without one gets the phone stack and no emphasis.

Turn

ucb-data--turn

The column names turn on their side so every column fits a phone: nothing scrolls, nothing hides, the grid stays a grid.

Honest: Long cell text wraps narrow, and past four columns the cells get too thin to read.

04Quick Answer, four looks

Quick Answer

One bold answer sentence, then two to four points, always. In the DOM in every look: the label, a <p><strong>, a <ul>, in that order, nothing behind a tap. Each frame is the proof article's hero, its answer and its opening.

Pick: Deck. Title and answer are one glance, above the fold, on the grain, and the answer sits straight after the H1 in the HTML, which is where an answer engine wants it. Statement is the safe second.

Statement

ucb-qa--statement

The answer is the biggest sentence on the page after the title, set the way the homepage sets its opening line, with the points as fine print under it.

Honest: A 44px sentence makes the page open slower; it suits answers of one line or two, not three.

Deck

Pickucb-qa--deck

The answer is the title's deck: it sits in the hero under the H1 on the category ground, so title and answer are one glance.

Honest: The hero gets tall, and the description line gives way to it. The page renders the answer in a different place for this look.

Margin

ucb-qa--margin

On a wide screen the answer stands in the margin beside the opening, a note that is there the whole time you read; under 1200px its label runs in ahead of the answer like a newspaper lead-in.

Honest: Only becomes a margin note from 1200px; on a laptop and a phone it is the run-in.

Grid

ucb-qa--grid

The answer is the lead cell of the service page's crossed grid and every point gets a cell of its own, read at a glance the way the problems are.

Honest: The widest look, past the reading column on desktop; with three points one cell sits empty.

05Workings, three looks

Workings

The sum behind a claim: every line ends in a bold value, a line opening "= " is the total. The build works out the sign between the lines from the numbers, writes it into the text, and stops if a sum doesn't hold. The signs are drawn as the site's grain cross. Copy: the proof article's two sums, then the Google Ads example (each line carries its own sum).

Pick: Worked. It holds every shape of sum on the site, chained or self-contained, and reads like a page of workings on a phone as well as a desk. Equation is the louder one and wraps on a phone.

Worked

Pickucb-work--worked

The sum set the way you'd do it on paper: figures stacked and right-aligned in light numerals, the sign beside them, a rule under the last line, the answer in the hero's stripes.

Honest: On a phone a long label wraps to three lines beside its figure.

Tally

ucb-work--tally

The result is kept as you go: each line carries the running figure on the right, growing with every line, so you watch 10 become 480 become $38,400.

Honest: Only a chained sum has a running figure; the Google Ads lines each carry their own sum, so there it is a plain list.

Equation

ucb-work--equation

The sum is one line across the column, each figure captioned underneath the way the homepage captions its stats, the answer last in the stripes.

Honest: It wraps on a phone, and a figure with words in it ("$80 an hour") takes a line to itself.
