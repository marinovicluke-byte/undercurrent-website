## Claim 2

> The 5 minute rule is the observation that response time is the single biggest factor in whether an inbound lead converts.

**Verdict:** PASS (paraphrased).
**Source:** Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," Harvard Business Review 2011, based on Oldroyd MIT Sloan / InsideSales 2007 study of 15,000+ leads across 100+ companies. [HBR summary cited widely](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads).
**Note:** "Single biggest factor" is a slight strengthening of the underlying research, which shows response time is the strongest operational lever in the Oldroyd dataset but does not claim exclusivity over all factors. Acceptable as industry shorthand, the phrasing is common in lead-response literature. No change needed.

## Claim 3

> A lead contacted within 5 minutes is significantly more likely to qualify than one contacted an hour later.

**Verdict:** PASS (paraphrased, conservative).
**Source:** Same Oldroyd study. The original finding: leads contacted within 5 minutes are 21x more likely to qualify than leads contacted after 30 minutes, and 100x more likely to connect. "Significantly more likely" is directionally correct and understated against the primary source. No change needed.

## Claim 4

> Google Ads generates leads through search campaigns, lead form extensions and Performance Max.

**Verdict:** DRIFT.
**Source:** [Google Ads Help, "About lead form assets"](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9423234). All major third-party references 2023 onward use "lead form assets".
**Correction:** Google renamed "lead form extensions" to "lead form assets" as part of the broader 2022 rollover of all ad extensions into the assets taxonomy. Use "lead form assets" in the copy. The feature itself is unchanged, only the naming drifted.

## Claim 5

> Physios, chiros, osteos, dentists and mobile clinics.

**Verdict:** DRIFT (category-mismatch).
**Source:** [Allied Health Professions Australia, "What is allied health"](https://www.ahpa.com.au/what-is-allied-health). [healthdirect.gov.au — Allied health](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/allied-health).
**Correction:** In the Australian definition, allied health explicitly excludes doctors, dentists and nurses. Dentists are registered under the Dental Board of Australia as a separate profession. Listing "dentists" inside an Allied Health card misaligns the page with the allied-health vertical it is targeting. Drop "dentists" from this card and replace with an actual allied health category (podiatrists, dietitians, psychologists, or speech pathologists all fit the inbound-lead, small-practice profile).

Suggested replacement:
> Physios, chiros, osteos, podiatrists and mobile clinics.

Dentists are a valid ICP for inbound lead management but belong under their own grouping or a broader "Health Practices" card. For this page, removing them cleans up the keyword targeting for "allied health" queries.
